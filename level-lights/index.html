Your level's lights

The lights already in your level take part in every mood. Tell Mood Kit what each one is, and it treats it that way.

What a light can be

Follow moodDims, tints and fails with the mood. The default.
Never failsFollows the mood but never flickers or dies. Tag: MoodSteady
FlameFlickers like fire and keeps its own warm colour. Tag: MoodFlame
AlarmPulses slowly, about once every 2 seconds. Tag: MoodAlarm
FaultyStutters every few seconds, more often than the rest. Tag: MoodFaulty
Leave aloneKeeps its own value. The mood leaves it alone. Tag: MoodIgnore

Choose what a light does

  1. Select one or more lights in the level or the Outliner.
  2. Click a role under Lights on the Place tab. The status line says what changed, and the line under the buttons counts your lights.

Roles undo like any edit. They are saved as actor tags, so you can also type a tag yourself. A Mood Lamp gets its role from its style, so a Candle is a Flame and a Beacon is an Alarm, unless you pick another.

Lights a mood cannot change

Static lights and baked light cannot change while a game runs. A mood still changes the sky, fog, colour and exposure on a baked level, and the status line tells you how many lights it cannot change. Set lights to Stationary or Movable for the strongest result.

Lamp meshes

A lamp mesh is dimmed with its light when the mesh is small and close to the light, or in the same actor. A lamp mesh with no collision, in a separate actor, stays bright.

Lights added later

  • A light you add in the editor joins the mood within a second.
  • Levels that stream in later change their sky, fog and colour at once. Their lamps join when you call Refresh Mood Lights after the level has loaded.

Pools of light a mood adds

Moods such as Failing Power, Red Alert and Long Dusk bring up to 4 pools of lamplight of their own, but only on levels with fewer than 3 lamps and no Mood Lamps. They stay ahead of the player. To never add them, untick Added lamps on the Fit tab.
