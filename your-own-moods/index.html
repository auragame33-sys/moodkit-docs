Moods

Make your own mood

Every value of a mood is yours to change, in your own copy. Your moods live in MoodKit > MyMoods, apart from the pack, so an update never touches them.

Save as My Mood

The quick way. Set the mood up on the Fit tab, then click Save as My Mood at the bottom of it.

  1. Pick a mood on the Moods tab, for example Drowned.
  2. Fit it. Move the sliders, pick a weather and turn its amounts up or down. Untick the parts you do not want.
  3. Click Save as My Mood at the bottom of the Fit tab. A new card, named like My Drowned, appears at the end of the cards.
  4. Use it like any other mood, on any level.

What goes into your mood, and what stays with the level:

Goes into the moodStays with the level
Brightness, Level lamps, Fog and Surface wearThe parts you ticked or unticked
The weather, and how much of its rain, snow and ash, lightning and windThe wind's direction
A sun or moon you aimed by handThe sun aim on this level
The parts you unticked that only add something: fog bank, surface wear, dust and weather, lens effects, added lamps and player lightThe parts that keep your level's own sky, sun, sky light, height fog, colour grade or lamps, and Branch shadows, because they depend on each level

On a mood of your own, Save as My Mood saves into that mood instead of making a new one.

Customize This Mood

For every value. Pick the mood closest to what you want, then click Customize This Mood on the Place tab.

  1. A copy is made in MoodKit > MyMoods, with its own card at the end, named like Failing Power Custom.
  2. A window opens with every value of the mood, in 12 groups: Card, Sky, Fog, Cast Shadows, Surface Wear, Dust and Embers, Weather, Lens, Lamps and Exposure, Added Lamps, Player Light and Picture.
  3. Change any value. Each one says what it does when you point at it, and the preview follows your edits within a second.

Every value is listed, with its range and help, in Mood values.

Colours mean what they look like

Colours and levels in a mood mean what they look like on screen, because the mood sets the exposure itself. A value of 0.18 reads as middle grey on any level, however it was lit.

Delete a mood of your own

Close the panel first. Then, in MoodKit > MyMoods, right-click the mood, for example DA_Drowned_Mine, and choose Delete.

Your own weathers and lamp styles

The same idea works for weathers and lamp styles. They live in MyWeather and MyLampStyles.
